# pro — legal / contract-review professionals

In-house counsel, contract managers, and paralegals. The single cleanest
fit of any newly-identified audience: legal citation-grounding is
structurally identical to the package-existence check already specced for
developers — this pack reuses the verification pipeline almost unmodified.

## Landscape and evidence

Current tools: Harvey (general legal AI, enterprise), Spellbook
(contract drafting/review, Word plugin), Ironclad and LinkSquares
(CLM platforms with AI review), CoCounsel (Thomson Reuters/Casetext), Robin
AI — a large, well-funded, fast-moving category.

Legal is unusually well-evidenced because failures become public court
record, not just forum complaints:

- **_Mata v. Avianca_** (S.D.N.Y. 2023) — attorneys sanctioned for filing a
  brief with six fabricated case citations generated by ChatGPT, none of
  which existed. This is now the canonical citation-hallucination case,
  and several federal judges now maintain standing orders requiring
  attorneys to certify AI-generated filings were checked against real case
  law.
- **ABA Formal Opinion 512** (2024) establishes that a lawyer's duty of
  "technological competence" explicitly extends to understanding
  generative-AI hallucination risk before relying on it — malpractice/
  professional-conduct exposure is now a named issue, not just an
  efficiency concern.
- Practitioner complaints converge on a specific gap: contract-review tools
  flag surface-level clause deviations well, but are unreliable at
  confirming whether a defined term is used *consistently* with its
  definition across a long agreement, or whether a clause is enforceable
  given jurisdiction-specific case law — both require grounding beyond the
  document itself.

## "pro" design — reuse, not new architecture

1. **Citation-grounding is the package-existence gate, applied to case
   law.** Before any case citation appears in output, verify it resolves
   against a live, authoritative case-law database (CourtListener/Westlaw/
   Lexis API) — the identical "verify against a live registry before
   presenting" pattern already built for npm/PyPI packages in
   [02-security-core.md §3](02-security-core.md). This is not a new
   mechanism, it's the same mechanism with a different registry.
2. **Defined-term/cross-reference grounding** is the contract-law analog
   of the AST/LSP symbol-grounding gate ([02-security-core.md §4](02-security-core.md)):
   a contract is a structured document with defined terms, cross-references,
   and exhibits — a deterministic parser verifies every use of a
   capitalized defined term matches its definition section and every
   cross-reference ("as set forth in Section 4.2") resolves to a real
   section, exactly as symbol resolution verifies a function call resolves
   to a real definition.
3. **Confidence signals/resampling** apply to clause-risk scoring (is this
   indemnification clause standard or unusually one-sided) — flag
   disagreement across resamples rather than presenting one confident risk
   score, reusing the SelfCheckGPT-style mechanism from
   [02-security-core.md §4](02-security-core.md) unchanged.
4. **Orthogonal review** — a second, differently-sourced pass specifically
   hunting for silent inconsistency between a clause and its cross-referenced
   definition, or between this contract and a counterparty's redline
   history — the same "genuinely different reviewer catches disjoint
   errors" principle from the code-review modes in
   [03-audience-developers-builders.md](03-audience-developers-builders.md).
5. **Audit log as compliance requirement, not just safety feature.** Legal
   work already requires defensible audit trails (privilege logs,
   engagement records) — the capability inventory view
   ([02-security-core.md §1](02-security-core.md)) maps directly onto "what
   did the AI actually touch in this matter," which outside-counsel
   guidelines increasingly ask about explicitly.
6. **Sandbox scope is narrow by nature**: contract drafting/redlining is
   read/write on a bounded document set (this matter's folder) with
   essentially no `run`/`net` surface — this audience exercises the
   capability model's narrowest slice, not its full breadth.

## Net-new work

Two connectors, both reusing existing verification mechanics rather than
inventing new ones: a legal-citation verification connector (CourtListener/
Westlaw/Lexis API) and a contract-structure parser (defined terms/
cross-references/exhibits). No new sandbox, cost, or orchestration
architecture required.
